The Challenge

When an Old Fence Cannot Hold Up to North Texas Conditions

A deteriorating fence does more than drag down curb appeal along the property line. Leaning posts, warped pickets, and gaps at the bottom let pets slip through, reduce privacy from neighboring views, and leave yards exposed to the intense sun and sudden storms that roll across Collin County every season. Many Plano homeowners live with fencing that was installed on shallow footings in heavy clay soil, built from materials never meant for triple-digit summers, or laid out without accounting for the drainage patterns common across lots from West Plano to East Plano.

Commercial property managers face a different set of pressures. Perimeter security, clear sight lines for customers, and durable gates that open smoothly under daily use all matter when the fence is the first boundary between your operation and the public. A bent chain link section or a gate that binds after a rainstorm sends the wrong message and creates avoidable liability.

Whether you need a backyard privacy screen in Lakeside on Preston, a pool safety enclosure near Arbor Hills Nature Preserve, or a full perimeter for a retail site along the Dallas North Tollway, the right fence should be planned for your soil, sun exposure, and long-term maintenance preferences. It should not be pulled from a generic catalog without regard for how the line actually runs across your ground.

Fence installation in Plano TX covers a wide range of needs. Some homeowners want a simple side-yard partition between neighbors. Others need a full backyard enclosure with a double gate wide enough for lawn equipment and a single walk gate near the patio. Pool owners require barriers that meet safety expectations with self-closing hardware and latch heights that keep children from accessing the water unsupervised. Each scenario calls for different materials, post spacing, and hardware, and we help you compare those options before any digging starts.

Wood remains one of the most popular choices across the Plano metro because it balances cost, appearance, and customization. Horizontal slat designs create a modern look along contemporary homes in new developments, while traditional shadowbox and stockade profiles suit established neighborhoods from Preston Meadow to Haggard Park. Staining or sealing schedules depend on sun exposure and moisture at the fence line, and we explain what upkeep to expect so you can choose confidently.

Vinyl fencing appeals to buyers who prefer a uniform appearance year-round without refinishing. Quality PVC profiles resist splintering, will not rot like untreated wood, and wipe clean after storms. Ornamental aluminum and wrought-iron styles add decorative character at entry points, along front landscapes, and around courtyard gardens where visibility matters as much as containment.

Chain link remains the practical standard for commercial and institutional sites across Collin County. Schools, warehouses, utility yards, and apartment complexes depend on durable mesh, tight tension, and gates that cycle reliably. We install top rails, tension bars, and terminal posts to manufacturer specifications so the fabric stays taut and the line reads professional from the street.

Gate installation is often where fence projects succeed or fail in daily use. A gate that sags, binds, or will not latch defeats the purpose of the entire run. We size posts for gate weight, specify hinges rated for outdoor duty, and align latches so they engage cleanly whether you choose a manual thumb latch or automated opener for a driveway entry.

FAQ

Common Questions About Fence Installation in Plano TX

How long does a typical fence installation take?
Most residential privacy and picket projects take one to three days depending on total linear footage, gate count, and soil conditions. Larger commercial runs or projects requiring extensive grading may take longer. We provide a schedule during your estimate so you know when crews will be on site.
Do I need a permit for a new fence in Plano?
Permit requirements depend on fence height, location on the lot, and whether you live within city limits or an HOA-governed subdivision. We help you understand local setback rules and height limits during the consultation and can advise on documentation when a permit is required.
Which fence material is best for North Texas weather?
Each material has trade-offs. Treated wood offers natural appearance with periodic sealing. Vinyl resists rot and needs minimal upkeep in hot, humid conditions. Chain link handles wind well on long commercial runs. We recommend options based on your sun exposure, moisture at the fence line, and how much maintenance you prefer.
Can you repair or replace sections of an existing fence?
Yes. We replace storm-damaged panels, reset leaning posts, swap worn gates, and extend existing lines when property boundaries change. Call (325) 331-8153 to describe the damage and we will recommend repair or full replacement.
